# RaceSafe design

**Data model.** Participants enroll users in a hackathon. A recruitment record starts as a draft and becomes a team at the minimum size. PostgreSQL derives draft status from the member count, bounds the maximum, and prevents finalization below the minimum. Membership references the participant and team in the same hackathon. A unique (hackathon_id, user_id) reserves one membership per person, including drafts. Join requests, invite digests, idempotency responses, and audit events have separate tables.

**Seat claims.** Invite redemption and leader acceptance share one transaction. It locks the applicant's participant row, then the team row, checks permissions and the current request or invite, and conditionally increments the counter only when an unlocked seat exists. Inserting the membership, marking the accepted request, cancelling the user's other pending requests, writing audit events, and scheduling live invalidation are part of that transaction. Errors roll back the reserved seat. Leader changes and last-member deletion lock the team row too. The oldest remaining member wins succession, with user ID breaking ties.

**Retries.** A user-scoped idempotency key serializes matching create, redeem, request, and accept operations. The response commits with the operation. A different payload with the same key is rejected. CI releases 50 invite requests and 50 approvals in separate races. The crash test kills the API after it reserves a seat, then verifies rollback and same-key recovery after restart.

**Realtime and audit.** Committed PostgreSQL notifications invalidate board snapshots and prompt React to refetch. Clients refetch after reconnect and discard displayed invites as soon as their version changes. The leader inbox removes approved requests optimistically and restores them on conflict. Audit entries capture actor, time, and field changes. Cursor pagination exposes the complete history to current members and the last member of a deleted team.

**Security and ranking.** Argon2id stores passwords. Expiring bearer tokens authenticate requests. Team writes check permissions inside transactions. Invites expire after 24 hours and have 60 bits of entropy. A separate PostgreSQL transaction stores per-user and per-IP rate limits across processes and counts failed guesses. Suggestions weight missing or novel skills at 70% and circular UTC offset proximity at 30%.

**Trade-offs.** The first board page has a one-second snapshot cache and a 30-second enrollment cache. The SSE service queues at most one pending invalidation per subscriber. Metrics cover request latency, response status, and streams. With another week, add IANA time zones and availability, target live notifications more narrowly, and measure the deployment under simultaneous browsing and team writes.
